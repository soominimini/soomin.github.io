Study Context

Not a lab study. An active clinic.

For 6 continuous months, a socially assistive robot ran in a live pediatric speech therapy center — operated independently by certified Speech-Language Pathologists (SLPs) in real clinical sessions with children with language challenges No researcher was in the room. Therapists controlled the robot themselves, during actual therapy, with real children.

This distinguishes the study from the vast majority of HRI research, which is conducted in controlled lab settings with researcher-operated systems. Here, clinicians were the operators, the evaluators, and the users whose needs ultimately determined whether the system succeeded.

The QT robot deployed in KickStart Therapy — operated by SLPs during live clinical sessions, no researcher present.

Design Process

Built with clinicians, not for them

The system emerged from 3 years of co-design with SLPs — iterative sessions focused on three core questions: What does SLPs need for their therapies? How should the robot interact with the SLP and children? And when does AI-generated content fail the child in the room?

These sessions revealed that therapists don't think in terms of system parameters — they think in terms of goals for a specific child on a specific day. Designing around this required shifting from feature-based UI to goal-oriented authoring.

The therapist-facing interface — designed for independent operation without engineering support. Each game maps to a clinical goal defined by the SLP.

Key Findings

What the deployment revealed

The deployment surfaced failure modes and design requirements invisible in shorter lab-based studies.

Interaction Fatigue

Children disengaged not because of the robot, but because pre-scripted AI content failed to adapt to their evolving goals and familiarity. Novelty decays; content must not.

Clinician Mental Models

SLPs developed accurate intuitions about when AI content would fail for a specific child — but had no mechanism to act on that knowledge. They needed authoring control, not just operation control.

Researcher Dependency as Structural Failure

Systems that require researcher involvement to update content cannot scale clinically. Sustainability requires non-technical users to be first-class content authors.

Successful Independent Operation

After onboarding, therapists successfully operated the robot system in live clinical sessions without engineering support — validating the core usability design.

Research Impact

What changed because of this deployment

The findings directly motivated a pivot in research direction. Pre-scripted systems, no matter how well-designed, cannot sustain clinical deployment. The question shifted: how do you give non-technical domain experts authoring control over AI behavior?

→ Ongoing Work

This deployment is the empirical foundation for an LLM-powered authoring system that enables SLPs to generate, customize, and iterate on AI-driven therapy content without researcher or engineering intervention — replacing rigid pre-scripted interactions with clinician-authored, LLM-executed ones.
